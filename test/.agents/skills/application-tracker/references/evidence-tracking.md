# Evidence: Follow-Up Timing and Tracking

Use in **drafts** only (`[^n]` plus a definition line from the Bibliography). Do not cite in emails the user sends.

## Rules and limits
- **There is no research on the effect of tracking an application.** Tracking is organisational advice. Say so.
- Follow-up timing comes from career-advice websites, not controlled studies. Present it as "commonly advised". The employer's stated timeline always overrides it.
- Sources tagged `[snippet]` were read from search results only.

## Findings
1. After applying, if there is no reply after one or two weeks, a follow-up email is a professional way to ask where the process stands [1][snippet]. — Low-Medium
2. Another source advises waiting five to seven business days after submitting before following up, and sending one follow-up only [2][snippet]. — Low (vendor blog)
3. After an interview, wait until any employer-stated response period has passed. If none was given, about five business days is the common default [3][4][snippet]. — Low-Medium
4. A thank-you note within about 24 hours (some sources say 24-48 hours) is commonly advised [5][6][snippet]. — Low-Medium
5. One source suggests a maximum of two follow-ups total after an interview [7][snippet]. — Low (vendor blog)

**Implication:** the tracker's default reminder (7 calendar days) is a reasonable middle of the commonly advised range. Treat it as a prompt and adjust it per employer.
