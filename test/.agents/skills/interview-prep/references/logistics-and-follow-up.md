## 1. Interview formats
Confirm with the recruiter: format (phone, video, on-site, panel, assessment centre), length, interviewers' names and roles, and whether there is a technical, case or presentation component. Ask what to prepare.

**AI-led or asynchronous (recorded) interviews.** In Greenhouse's 2025 survey, 54% of US job seekers had met an AI-led interview [17][opened]. If you get one:
- Read the disclosure about recording, how the recording and any scoring are used, and how long it is kept. Ask the recruiter if it is unclear.
- Ask what the format is (time per answer, retakes) and whether a human alternative exists.
- Practise on camera with a timer. Answer the same way you would for a person: specific stories, numbers, no script.
- Do not use live-assist tools or have anyone feed you answers (see §D in `evidence-interviewing.md`). Check the employer's stated policy on tools.

**Reasonable adjustments.** You may ask for a change to the process (extra time, questions in advance, a different format, a breakout break) without disclosing a diagnosis. You choose how much to say. In the UK the duty to adjust applies when the employer knows, or could reasonably be expected to know, about a disability, so a short statement that you have a condition affecting X may be needed [18][19][snippet]. Rules differ by country: check locally. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

## 2. Research checklist
- The job brief (use `job-ad-analyzer` if there is no brief yet).
- The company: products, customers, strategy, recent news (12 months), competitors, leadership.
- The interviewers: role and background from professional, company-published material only (for example the company website or a professional profile they have chosen to publish). Do not store personal data about interviewers beyond what the user supplied, and do not contact them. Never mention personal details.
- The team: size, reporting line, the problem the role solves.
- The market: typical pay range for the role (see `salary-negotiation`).
- Practicalities: location or link, dress norms, time zone, documents to bring.

## 3. Day-before checklist
- [ ] Story bank reviewed; top 6 stories said aloud once.
- [ ] 3-5 questions for the interviewer written down.
- [ ] CV and job brief printed or open.
- [ ] Route, parking or link tested; arrival buffer planned.
- [ ] Clothing ready; ID or documents packed if required.
- [ ] Sleep and food planned.

## 4. Virtual interview checklist
- Test camera, microphone, light, background and the platform link in advance.
- Camera at eye level; stable connection; wired if possible; phone as a backup.
- Close notifications and unrelated tabs.
- Keep notes beside the screen, not a script.
- Tell the interviewer if the connection drops, and have a backup phone number ready.

## 5. During the interview
- Greet by name, with a friendly start. Whether to shake hands, bow, nod or use another greeting depends on the culture; follow the interviewer's lead.
- Listen to the whole question. Pause. Ask for clarification if needed.
- Use the story bank; keep to the time.
- Take brief notes on names and topics for the follow-up.
- Ask your questions. Close with interest in the role and a question about next steps.
- Ask for the interviewers' contact details or confirm the recruiter will pass the thanks on.

## 6. After the interview
- Write down what was asked and how you answered, while it is fresh.
- Send a thank-you within about 24-48 hours [15].
- If the employer gave a timeline, wait until it passes. If not, about five business days is the commonly advised wait before one follow-up [13][14].
- Send no more than two follow-ups in total, then move on.
- Update the tracker (`application-tracker`).

## 7. Thank-you note template (3-5 sentences)
```
Subject: Thank you - [Role] interview

Hi [Name],

Thank you for taking the time to speak with me today about the [Role]. I particularly enjoyed discussing [specific topic], and it reinforced my interest in [team goal].

[One new thought or answer you wish you had added: a relevant example or a point from the conversation.]

I look forward to the next steps. Please let me know if I can provide anything else.

Best regards,
[Name]
```
Rules: personalise one detail; keep it short; check names and spelling; do not restate the whole CV.

## 8. Follow-up template (after the stated timeline)
```
Subject: Following up - [Role]

Hi [Name],

I hope you are well. I wanted to follow up on the [Role] position we discussed on [date]. I remain very interested, and I would be glad to share anything else that would help. Could you let me know where things stand?

Thank you,
[Name]
```

## 9. Handling rejection and feedback
- Thank them and ask politely for feedback; many will not give it, and that is normal.
- Record lessons: which questions were weak, which stories need work.
- Keep the relationship warm; roles reopen.
- Update the story bank and the tracker.
