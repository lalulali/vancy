# Teaching, Mock Interviews, Scoring and Answer Improvement

Citation numbers refer to `evidence-interviewing.md`. Use this file when teaching a candidate how to structure an answer, running a live mock interview, scoring a spoken or written answer, or rewriting a weak answer from the candidate's own facts.

For Product Manager case questions (product sense, strategy, analytical/metrics, execution, technical system design, estimation, AI PM), hand off to `pm-interview-prep`. Keep behavioural ("tell me about a time"), motivation, situational and sensitive questions here.

## 1. Teaching how to answer ("I do -> we do -> you do")

Never hand the candidate a script to memorise [1]. Teach the repeatable structure so they can adapt under pressure:
1. **Decode the question (15 seconds):** State what competency or risk the interviewer is actually testing (for example, "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a manager" tests influence without authority, emotional maturity, and whether you back down or escalate constructively).
2. **Show the blueprint ("I do"):** Walk through the 4-step structure for that question family (§2) and show a short outline from `story-bank.md` or a contrasting weak vs. strong move, explaining *why* the strong move works.
3. **Guided build ("we do"):** Ask the candidate 2-3 mining questions to pull their real facts, decisions and numbers (marking any unknown figure `[metric?]`). Have them draft or speak the outline step by step.
4. **Timed delivery ("you do"):** Ask the question cold, let them answer without interruption, then score (§4) and run one improvement cycle (§5).

## 2. Answer blueprints by question family

### A. Opening & motivation ("Tell me about yourself", "Why this company/role?")
- **Tell me about yourself (60-90 seconds, ~150-220 words):**
  1. **Present (20-30s):** Current role, scope, and one headline proof point relevant to the target job.
  2. **Past (25-35s):** Two or three deliberate career highlights or transitions that built the skills this role needs.
  3. **Future (15-20s):** Why this company and role are the logical next step, tied to the problem in the Job Brief.
- **Why this company? (45-60 seconds):**
  1. One verifiable fact about the company's product, customers, stage or strategy (never generic flattery).
  2. How that connects to a problem you have solved before, with one proof point.
  3. What you want to help them achieve in the first year.

### B. Behavioural ("Tell me about a time..." -> STAR + L) [5][7][9]
Target **1-2 minutes spoken (~150-280 words)**:
- **S - Situation (10-15%):** 1-2 sentences setting the stakes (company/team context and why the problem mattered).
- **T - Task (10-15%):** One sentence on what *you* were accountable for and the constraint (deadline, budget, conflict, ambiguity).
- **A - Action (50-60%):** 3-4 concrete steps *you* took. Name the decision you made, the alternative you rejected, how you handled pushback or risk, and who you collaborated with. Use "I" for your actions and "we" for the shared outcome (adjusting tone for team-oriented markets; see `story-bank.md` §4).
- **R - Result + Learning (20-25%):** Quantified outcome or clear before/after change, followed by one sentence on what you learned or what process you changed afterwards.

### C. Situational ("What would you do if...") [11][12]
Target **90-120 seconds**:
1. **Clarify & state the goal:** Name the constraint or ask 1-2 quick clarifying questions; state the primary objective you are protecting.
2. **Assess options & stakeholders:** Lay out 2-3 realistic options and the trade-offs for customers, the team, and the business.
3. **Choose & sequence:** Pick a clear first action, a short-term mitigation, and a longer-term fix.
4. **Measure & verify:** Name the signal or metric that tells you the plan worked, plus the main risk.
5. **Anchor in real experience:** Close with 1-2 sentences connecting to a real past situation ("I ran into a similar constraint at X, where I...").

### D. Tricky & sensitive ("Greatest weakness", "A failure", "Why the gap/layoff?")
- **Failure / mistake:** Pick a real, non-catastrophic decision or oversight you owned -> state the impact honestly without blaming others -> show how you fixed or contained it -> end with the specific safeguard or habit you built so it has not recurred.
- **Weakness:** Name a genuine, work-related skill or habit that is not a hard filter for the role -> give a brief example of how it showed up -> explain the concrete system or practice you use to manage it -> share recent evidence of progress.

## 3. Mock interview protocol and interviewer follow-up probes

### Running the mock
1. **Set the stage:** Confirm the mode:
   - *Quick diagnostic (15-20 min):* 3 questions (1 opener, 1 core behavioural, 1 failure/conflict or situational) to spot habits.
   - *Full round (30-45 min):* 5-6 questions mapped to the Job Brief's top requirements, plus 5 minutes at the end for the candidate's questions to the interviewer.
   - *Format simulation:* 1:1 hiring manager, cross-functional peer, panel (2-3 distinct interviewer voices), or async recorded video (strict 90-120s timer, no follow-ups).
2. **One question at a time:** Ask the prompt and wait for the candidate's complete answer. Never coach or hint mid-answer.
3. **Probe like a real interviewer:** Structured interviewers rarely accept the surface story; they ask 1-3 follow-up probes to test depth and veracity. Pick from the probe bank below based on what the candidate left vague:

| What was thin | Interviewer follow-up probe |
|---|---|
| Ownership ("we" fog) | "Walk me through what *you* personally decided versus what your manager or team did." |
| Alternatives & judgement | "What other options did you consider, and why did you rule them out?" |
| Conflict / pushback | "Who disagreed with your approach, what was their best argument, and how did you respond?" |
| Evidence / metrics | "How did you measure that outcome? How do you know your change caused it?" |
| Technical / craft depth | "Take me one level deeper into how you actually built / analysed / executed that step." |
| Hindsight & learning | "If you had to run that project again tomorrow with half the time, what would you do differently?" |
| Scale / stakes | "What would have happened to the team or customer if you hadn't stepped in?" |

4. **Step out of role explicitly** before giving scores and rewrites.

## 4. Behavioural and general interview scoring rubric (1-5)

Score every practised answer on five weighted dimensions (1 = weak, 3 = solid/hireable, 5 = exceptional). Quote a phrase from the candidate's answer as evidence for each score. Be candid: most first drafts score 2.0-3.2.

| Dimension | Weight | 1 (Weak) | 3 (Solid) | 5 (Exceptional) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Relevance & fit** | 20% | Answers a different question; story is too junior or trivial for the target level | Directly answers the prompt; story matches the target competency and level | Directly answers the prompt and naturally proves a top requirement from the Job Brief |
| **2. Structure & pacing** | 20% | Rambling; >40% spent on backstory; missing Task or Result; >3 min or <45s | Clear STAR/blueprint flow; setup <=30%; ~1-2 min (~150-280 words) | Crisp 2-sentence setup, signposted actions, punchy result; lands in 90-120s |
| **3. Ownership & decisions** | 25% | Heavy "we" or passive duty list ("was responsible for"); no personal decision shown | Clear "I" actions; names at least one concrete decision or obstacle handled | Explains *why* key decisions were made, trade-offs weighed, and how others were influenced |
| **4. Impact & evidence** | 20% | Vague outcome ("it went well"); no numbers, before/after, or observable change | Credible metric, before/after comparison, or concrete observable outcome | Quantified impact with baseline and timeframe, tied to business or customer value |
| **5. Reflection & composure** | 15% | No lesson learned; defensive or evasive on follow-up probes | Clear 1-line takeaway; answers follow-up probes directly | Insightful changed behaviour applied later; handles tough probes with candour and poise |

**Weighted score** = `0.20*Relevance + 0.20*Structure + 0.25*Ownership + 0.20*Impact + 0.15*Reflection`.

**Practice bands (heuristic, not a hiring guarantee):**
- **< 2.5:** Needs rebuild (usually missing structure, personal action, or a real example).
- **2.5 - 3.3:** Developing (story is real, but setup is heavy or impact/decisions are vague).
- **3.4 - 4.1:** Interview-ready (clear STAR, clear ownership, quantified result, handles probes).
- **4.2 - 5.0:** Standout (tight pacing, non-obvious trade-offs, strong metric and reflection).

**Red flags (cap the affected dimension at 2.0 until fixed):**
- **Disguised strength** on a failure/weakness prompt ("I care too much / work too hard").
- **Blaming** colleagues, reports, customers, or a former employer.
- **No personal action** (cannot name a single decision or step the candidate took).
- **Invented or contradictory figures** (conflicts with the candidate's CV or earlier answers).
- **Memorised script delivery** that collapses when asked a follow-up probe.

## 5. "Make it better": before/after rewrite loop and fix library

After scoring an answer, run this 4-step improvement loop:
1. **What worked (1-2 bullets):** Name specific strengths to keep (e.g., "Your result had a clear 9-days-to-2-days baseline").
2. **Top 2-3 ranked fixes:** Focus on the highest-weight gaps first.
3. **Before -> After rewrite (from the candidate's own facts only):**
   - Quote the weakest section of their answer (*Before*).
   - Show a tightened version (*After*) that preserves their truth and voice, marking any missing number `[metric?]` and any missing detail `[fact?]`.
   - Explain in one line why the rewrite scores higher on the rubric.
4. **Immediate re-try:** Ask the candidate to re-deliver the tightened answer (or just the fixed section) in their own words, or hit them with one follow-up probe to lock it in.

### Common defects and how to fix them
| Defect | Symptom | Rewrite move |
|---|---|---|
| **The Backstory Trap** | 60%+ of words explain company history or org charts before the candidate acts | Cut Situation + Task to 2 sentences: `[Context + problem stake]` + `[My goal + constraint]`. Move straight to `"I started by..."` |
| **The "We" Fog** | `"We redesigned the flow and we aligned stakeholders"` | Split team context from personal action: `"As a squad of 4, our goal was X. I owned Y: I interviewed..., I proposed..., and I negotiated..."` |
| **The Duty List Action** | `"I was responsible for managing the project and attending weekly syncs"` | Replace routine duties with the 3 hardest moments: **Diagnose** (what you found) -> **Decide** (what trade-off you made) -> **Deliver** (how you executed/persuaded) |
| **The Unmeasured Result** | `"The launch went smoothly and stakeholders were happy"` | Ask: what changed in time, volume, error rate, revenue, adoption, or risk? Even a directional or estimated before/after (`"cut manual review from ~2 hours to 15 minutes [metric?: confirm]"`) beats adjectives |
| **The Missing Takeaway** | Story ends abruptly at the metric | Add one sentence: `"That taught me to [specific habit], which I later used when [brief application]."` |
| **The Humblebrag Failure** | `"My failure was taking on too much work myself"` | Swap for a real misjudgement (a wrong hypothesis, missed stakeholder, or late escalation) where the fix and lesson are concrete |
| **The Resume Recital Opener** | "Tell me about yourself" walks chronologically through every job since graduation | Compress into Present (role + 1 headline metric) -> Past (2 themes/wins that match the Job Brief) -> Future (why this role now) in 75 seconds |

## 6. Practice drills (5-10 minutes each)
- **Two-Sentence Setup Drill:** Take 4 stories from the bank and state S + T for each in under 20 seconds (max 45 words).
- **"I" Conversion Drill:** Re-tell one Action block starting every step with a concrete verb (`I analysed`, `I proposed`, `I cut`, `I escalated`) while naming collaborators accurately.
- **Probe Gauntlet:** Deliver one 90-second story, then answer 3 back-to-back follow-up probes (ownership, trade-off, metric) in under 45 seconds each.
- **Flex Drill:** Take one story from the bank and re-angle its emphasis to answer 3 different prompts (e.g., leadership, conflict, and ambiguity) without changing any facts.
- **60-Second Opener Drill:** Deliver "Tell me about yourself" + "Why this company?" back-to-back in under 2 minutes total.
