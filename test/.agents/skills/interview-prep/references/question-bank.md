# Question Bank and Answer Strategies

Use this to build a tailored practice list from the job brief. Do not hand the candidate scripted answers to memorise. Show the strategy, then have them build the answer from their own facts.

## 1. Opening and motivation
| Question | Strategy |
|---|---|
| "Tell me about yourself." | Present -> past -> future in about 60-90 seconds: what you do now and one proof; two or three relevant steps that got you here; why this role next. Not a life story |
| "Why this company?" | One specific, verifiable fact (product, strategy, news, mission) plus how your experience connects. Never flatter without a link to what you can contribute |
| "Why this role?" | Link the role's problem (from the job brief) to your strongest evidence |
| "Why are you leaving?" | Move toward something, not away from something. Short, factual, no blame |
| "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" | Honest direction that fits the role's growth path; show you want to deepen skills here |
| "What do you know about us?" | Two or three accurate facts from your research, then a question |

## 2. Behavioural prompts ("Tell me about a time...")
Use STAR from the story bank. Common prompts: led a team; disagreed with someone; made a mistake; handled pressure; influenced without authority; solved a hard problem; learned fast; worked with a difficult stakeholder; took initiative; delivered under limited information.

## 3. Situational prompts ("What would you do if...")
Structure: clarify -> assess options -> choose and explain -> state how you would measure success -> note a risk and mitigation. Use a past example if you have one ("I faced something similar when...") to show evidence, not just theory.

## 4. Role-specific and technical prompts
Build these from the job brief's ranked requirements.
- **Technical / craft:** be ready to explain a past project in depth (problem, constraints, choices, result).
- **Case / analytical:** state assumptions, structure the problem, think aloud, check the answer. Practise with timed examples.
- **Take-home or presentation:** confirm scope and time, state assumptions, show your reasoning, and keep to the brief.
- **Panel:** address the person who asked and acknowledge the others, using the eye-contact style that is normal in that culture and comfortable for you. You can request adjustments (see `logistics-and-follow-up.md` §1). Keep a note of names.
- **Async video:** check the tech, frame yourself well, keep to the time limit, and rehearse once.

## 5. Tricky and sensitive questions
| Question | Strategy |
|---|---|
| "What is your greatest weakness?" | Real, non-central weakness + what you do about it + progress |
| "Tell me about a failure." | Real, modest, with a changed behaviour (story bank §6) |
| "Why is there a gap in your CV?" | Brief, factual, then what you did or learned and why you are ready now |
| "Why were you let go / laid off?" | One factual sentence, no blame, then forward-looking |
| "What are your salary expectations?" | See the `salary-negotiation` skill. Ask about the range first if the employer has not shared one; if you give a figure, make it researched and a range |
| "Do you have other offers?" | Honest and brief; do not bluff. Express continued interest |
| "Are you overqualified?" | Show what you want from the role and how you will stay engaged. "Overqualified" can be a proxy for age, so answer the underlying concern (cost, flight risk, boredom, authority) with specifics: why this role, how long you plan to stay, how you work with a manager who is less experienced |
| "How do you handle feedback?" | A real example of changing your approach |

## 6. Questions the candidate should ask [16]
Pick 3-5 that the interview has not already answered:
- What does success look like in the first 90 days and the first year?
- What is the biggest challenge the team faces right now?
- How is performance measured and reviewed?
- How does the team make decisions and handle disagreement?
- What does the hiring process look like from here, and when should I expect to hear back?
- What do you enjoy most about working here?
- (When appropriate) Why is the role open?
Avoid questions answered on the careers page, and avoid opening with salary, benefits, or leave unless the employer does.

## 7. Unlawful or inappropriate questions
Rules differ by country. Questions about age, marital status, family plans, religion, nationality, health or similar topics can be restricted. A calm approach:
1. Answer the underlying job-related concern ("I can commit to the schedule and travel this role requires").
2. Or politely decline: "I'd prefer to keep the focus on my qualifications for the role."
3. If it persists, note it afterwards and consider reporting it to the employer or an official body.
Do not give legal advice; point to official sources for the relevant country.
