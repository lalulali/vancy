# Evidence: LinkedIn Profiles

Use in **drafts** only (cite as `[^n]`, definition line copied from the Bibliography). Never put citations in the profile copy itself.

## Rules and honest limits
- **The evidence base here is weak.** LinkedIn does not publish its recruiter-search ranking. Most "how the algorithm works" material is vendor or coaching blogs. Treat every ranking claim as low-confidence and tell the user so.
- Sources tagged `[snippet]` were read from search results only.
- Character limits are consistent across many third-party sources but I did not find them on a LinkedIn help page. **Tell the user to confirm them in the LinkedIn editor, which shows a live counter.**
- Never repeat invented precision such as "headline keywords are 40% of ranking" (finding 8).

## Findings

### A. How recruiters find people (direction only)
1. Recruiters run filtered searches by keywords, job title, skills, location and current company [1][snippet]. — Low-Medium (coaching blog describing LinkedIn's documented filters)
2. Keywords are matched across the whole profile, so the same terms should appear naturally in Headline, About, Experience and Skills rather than in the headline alone [2][snippet]. — Low
3. The headline shows in search results, comments and connection requests, so it is the most-seen line [3][snippet]. — Low-Medium
4. Completeness (all sections filled, measurable achievements, relevant skills) is repeatedly advised as improving discoverability [4][snippet]. — Low (compilation of member posts)
5. LinkedIn's Help page says Open to Work lets you choose who sees it: all members (which includes recruiters and people at your current company, and adds the photo frame), recruiters only (LinkedIn Recruiter users), or only you. For recruiters-only, LinkedIn takes steps to hide it from Recruiter users at your current company but says it cannot guarantee complete privacy [11][opened]. — High (official help page). **Recruiters-only reduces visibility, but LinkedIn cannot guarantee your employer won't see it. Remind users to read the current settings, which change.**

### B. Field limits (verify in product)
6. Headline 220, About 2,600, experience description 2,000, skill name 80 characters. Posts 3,000 [5][6][snippet]. — Medium (many consistent secondary sources)
7. Only the first lines of About show before "see more", so the opening carries most of the weight [7][snippet]. The exact cut-off varies by device. — Low-Medium

### C. Claims to avoid
8. A blog claims recruiters filter by "headline keyword density (40%), top 3 pinned skills (25%), recent activity (20%)" [8][snippet]. — **UNVERIFIED. No method or LinkedIn source. Do not repeat it.**

### D. Honesty and verification
9. In Greenhouse's 2025 report, hiring managers reported more checks on misrepresented experience and more in-person interviews [9]. — Medium-High. **A LinkedIn profile that contradicts the CV is easy to catch. Keep titles, employers and dates identical across both.**
10. In many places photos and personal attributes can influence decisions. The US EEOC lists the prohibited practices [10][snippet]. — Medium. **A photo is the user's choice. Do not insist on one, and do not advise on legal compliance.**
