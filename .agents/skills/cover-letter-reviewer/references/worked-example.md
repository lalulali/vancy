# Worked Review Example (Cover Letter)

A complete application of the rubric. The candidate and company are fictional; target role: **Senior Product Manager** at "Northwind Payments". All names and numbers in this example are fictional; never reuse them or any figure the user didn't give. Any resemblance to real people or companies is coincidental.

## Input (the letter under review)
```
Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to apply for the Senior Product Manager position I saw advertised.
I am a results-driven, passionate team player with excellent communication skills
and a proven track record. I have always dreamed of working at a renowned company
like yours.

In my current role I am responsible for the product roadmap and work with many
teams. I believe I would be a perfect fit for this role and it would be a great
opportunity for me to grow my career.

I have attached my CV. I hope to hear from you.

Yours faithfully,
Jon Smith
```

## Review output

## Any-company sentences (rewrite or cut)
- "I am a results-driven, passionate team player…"
- "I have always dreamed of working at a renowned company like yours."
- "I believe I would be a perfect fit… a great opportunity for me to grow."

## Top fixes (ranked)
1. **High: Name the company and a verifiable fact; address the ad.** In a field experiment, tailored letters got 53% more callbacks than no letter, while generic letters helped only slightly.[^16]
   - Before: "I am writing to apply for the Senior Product Manager position I saw advertised."
   - After: "When Northwind announced its move into instant payouts last month [fact?: confirm the Northwind news], I recognised a problem I've already solved: over [N] years [confirm: roles span 2018–present] in [fact?: confirm your domain, e.g. B2B payments] I've cut merchant onboarding from [metric?: baseline] days to [metric?: result] [fact?: confirm this result is yours]."
2. **High: Add one real, quantified result that matches the CV.**[^33]
   - After: "By [fact?: what you changed] I lifted checkout conversion from [metric?]% to [metric?]%, adding [metric?: revenue] a year."
3. **High: Flip from "me" to "you".** Replace "great opportunity for me to grow" with a first contribution, for example "in my first 60 days I'd audit your onboarding funnel against fraud signals" [fact?: confirm this is a plan you want to commit to].
4. **Medium: Replace the stock phrases.** Swap "results-driven / passionate / team player / proven track record" for one specific example each; generic content is a common rejection reason.[^8]
5. **Medium: Find the hiring manager's name** and open "Dear Ms …" / "Dear Mr …" (or the full name if you are unsure of the title). If truly unknown, use "Dear Hiring Manager," with "Kind regards," (modern convention, unsourced); keep "Yours faithfully," for "Dear Sir or Madam", as the National Careers Service pairs them.[^13]
6. **Medium: Reach 250–400 words** (the body is about 93) with a proof paragraph and a fit paragraph.

## What's working
- The first sentence names the target role, so the reader knows immediately what the letter is for.
- Polite, professional register with no typos, and the salutation and sign-off pair consistently.

## Questions for you
- What is the real onboarding/conversion/revenue figure?
- Is there recent Northwind news you can point to, and do you know the hiring manager's name?
