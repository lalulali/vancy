# Evidence: References

Use in **drafts** only (`[^n]` plus a definition line from the Bibliography). Do not cite in the final reference sheet or in messages sent to referees.

## Rules and limits
- **This evidence is career-advice guidance only.** I found no research on how references influence hiring. Say "commonly advised".
- Sources tagged `[snippet]` were read from search results only.
- Data-protection and employer-reference rules vary by country. The skill gives no legal advice.
- Never invent a referee, a title, a date or a quote.

## Findings
1. Choose references who can speak to the knowledge, skills and abilities relevant to the target position, and who can give concrete examples of your accomplishments [1][snippet]. — Low-Medium
2. Always ask permission before listing someone as a reference. A surprised referee tends to give a weaker, less enthusiastic endorsement [2][snippet]. — Low-Medium
3. Prepare a list of reliable contacts in advance, so you can provide it when an employer asks [3][snippet]. — Low-Medium
4. Some employers ask for the reference list later in the hiring process [4][snippet]. — Low-Medium
5. Avoid family members, personal friends and non-professional contacts, since they cannot give an objective assessment of work performance [5][snippet]. — Low-Medium
6. Typical referees: an immediate supervisor, manager or co-worker [6][snippet]. — Low-Medium (university career centre)
7. Say thank you afterwards [7][snippet]. — Low
8. Listing the relationship and details of your work together helps hiring managers and can speed up selection [4][snippet]. — Low-Medium
9. A resume does not need "References available upon request"; the space is better used for content [8]. — Low-Medium
10. Germany: employees have a statutory right to a written reference at the end of employment (§ 109 GewO) [9][snippet]. — Medium-High (statute text via a legal database)
11. UK financial services: the FCA's SYSC 22 regulatory-references regime covers at least the previous six years of employment [10][snippet]. — Medium-High (regulator handbook)
12. UK (other sectors), US, Gulf and Asia practices (factual-only references, dates-and-title confirmation, experience or relieving letters) are common advice but **unverified here**: say "check locally".
