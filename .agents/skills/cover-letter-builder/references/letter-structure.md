## 1. Layout (Markdown)
```markdown
---
doc: cover-letter
template: modern      # same as the CV
page: a4              # letter for US/CA
lang: en-GB
---

# Full Name
+44 7700 900123 | name@example.com | linkedin.com/in/name | City, Country

16 October 2026                 <- optional; use it for formal or postal letters. Date format follows the market (US: October 16, 2026)

Mr Daniel Okafor                <- optional recipient block (en-GB: no full stop after Mr/Ms/Dr; en-US: "Mr." / "Ms.")
Head of Product, Northwind

**Re: Senior Product Manager, Merchant Onboarding**

Dear Mr Okafor,                 <- en-GB. en-US: "Dear Mr. Okafor,". Unsure of the title or gender? Use the full name: "Dear Daniel Okafor,". Never infer it from a first name

P1 … P4/P5

Yours sincerely,                <- en-GB with a named recipient [13]. "Kind regards," is also common in modern UK business writing (convention, unsourced). Never "Yours faithfully," with a name. en-US: "Sincerely,"

Full Name
```
- All names and numbers here are fictional; never reuse them or any figure the user did not give. Salutations and sign-offs by market are in `regional-conventions.md`.
- Use the same header (name and contact line) as the CV, so the two read as a matched set. Contact details must be identical in both.
- Include contact details once, in the header. Repeating them in the closing paragraph is optional and must match exactly.
- The subject line goes **before** the salutation.

## 2. Paragraph rules
Aim for 250–400 words in the body, across 3–5 paragraphs [13][30].

**P1. Hook and thesis (50–80 words).**
- Open with a specific reason for applying to *this* company, or a strong proof point. Do not open with "I am writing to apply for…".
- Name the role and the company.
- Give a one-line career identity with years and specialism, plus a headline metric.
- End by stating the value you will bring.
- Keep the first sentence to 2 lines or fewer.

**P2. Proof from the current or most relevant role (60–110 words).**
- State your title, employer and scope (team, budget, markets, users).
- Cover 1–2 requirements from the ad, using its own terms.
- Put the strongest achievement **last** in the paragraph, as action → result → metric (STAR compressed) [31][38].
- If the numbers are confidential, use percentages or ranges.

**P3. Optional second proof (40–80 words).** Use one of:
- a previous role or project
- for a graduate: internship, thesis or leadership
- for a career changer: the transferable bridge
- for a returner: one honest gap line plus how you kept current

Add something new. Do not repeat the CV.

**P4. Fit and value (50–90 words).**
- Start from *their* world: a priority, challenge or goal taken from the job ad or your research.
- Show how a complementary skill or experience solves it, and name a concrete first contribution ("in my first 90 days…").
- A compliment is fine only when it leads to a contribution. Flattery for its own sake is a turn-off.
- Don't dwell on past employers here; look forward.

**P5. Call to action and close (25–50 words).**
- Show enthusiasm without desperation: invite a conversation about a specific topic.
- Thank the reader.
- No ultimatums ("I will call you on Monday") unless following up is customary and polite in that market.

## 3. By situation
| Situation | Adjust |
|---|---|
| Graduate | P1: degree plus your strongest internship or project result. P2: internship or leadership with numbers. P3: relevant modules or thesis. Never "this role would help me grow" without adding what you give in return |
| Senior / executive | Strategic framing: P&L, transformation, team building. Name one specific opportunity for the company. Prose only (no bullets). Executive template |
| Career changer | P1 leads with motivation and the bridge ("8 years in hospital operations taught me…"). P3 translates transferable wins into the target field's vocabulary. Show proof of commitment (a course, a project) |
| Returner | One factual line on the gap, then recent upskilling. Stay confident, not apologetic |
| Referral | Name the referrer in the first sentence (with permission) |
| Speculative | Lead with the company's need. Under 250 words. Ask for a short conversation |
| Bullets vs prose | Prose is the default. 2–3 bullets in P2 are fine for evidence-dense letters in sales or operations. Never use bullets for the whole letter |

## 4. Tone and style
- Warm and professional: personable, not over-formal and not chatty. Contractions are fine in conversational markets and startups; avoid them in formal or government letters.
- Follow the "you" test: after every sentence, ask "does this show what I can do for them?" If not, cut it.
- One spelling convention, matching the market and the CV.
- Avoid stock enthusiasm ("thrilled", "honoured"), clichés ("perfect fit", "team player"), and claims without proof.
- Humour or gimmicks only if the brand is clearly informal and the user explicitly wants it.
- Never add hidden text or prompt-injection text [32].

## 5. Final checklist
- [ ] Company name and job title appear in the subject line or P1. Recipient name spelled correctly.
- [ ] At least one verifiable company-specific fact, and at least one requirement from the job ad addressed explicitly.
- [ ] At least two quantified results that match the CV exactly.
- [ ] Body is 250–400 words (or within the form's limit). Fits on one page.
- [ ] The letter adds context; it is not a CV recap.
- [ ] Salutation and sign-off are correct for the market (see `regional-conventions.md`).
- [ ] Contact details are identical to the CV.
- [ ] No placeholders, citations or notes in the final.
