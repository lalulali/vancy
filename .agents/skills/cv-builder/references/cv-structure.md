# CV Structure and Section Rules

Source numbers refer to `evidence.md`.

## Contents
1. Choose a format · 2. Section order · 3. Header · 4. Professional Summary · 5. Key Skills · 6. Professional Experience · 7. Education & Certifications · 8. Additional Information · 9. Length & formatting

## 1. Choose a format
| Format | Use when | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse-chronological (default) | Steady history in one field | Recruiters and ATS read it most easily [4][42] |
| Hybrid / combination | Strong skills to foreground plus a real timeline; career changers with relevant history | Summary + skills/achievements up top, then dated experience |
| Functional (skills-only) | Rarely — only on request | Hides timeline; recruiters distrust it. Prefer hybrid + honest gap line |
| Academic CV | Faculty, research, postdoc, grants | Add Publications, Grants, Teaching, Presentations, Service; no page limit [45] |

## 2. Section order (default)
1. Header
2. Professional Summary
3. Key Skills
4. Professional Experience (each role: scope bullet → responsibilities → **Key Achievements**)
5. Education & Certifications (move above Experience for students/graduates or when a degree/licence is the key requirement)
6. Projects / Publications / Volunteering (if relevant)
7. Additional Information (languages, tools, work authorisation, availability — market-dependent)

Recruiters skim titles, employers, dates, then education first [4][23]; the top third of page one carries the most weight [24]. Put the strongest relevant evidence there.

## 3. Header
- Name (largest text). Optional headline under it: target job title (match the ad's title if it truthfully reflects your work) + 1–2 specialisms, e.g. `Senior Data Analyst | Pricing | Experimentation`.
- One contact line: phone (country code) | professional email | LinkedIn/portfolio | City, Country.
- Keep contact details in the body, never in Word header/footer [27].
- Exclude by default: photo, DOB, age, gender, marital status, religion, ID numbers, full address, salary — see `regional-conventions.md` [31][55].

## 4. Professional Summary
Write it **last**, after Experience — it distils the best evidence.
- 3–5 bullets or a 3–4-line paragraph (≈40–90 words).
- Line 1 = identity + years + domains + calibre: `[Target title] with [N]+ years in [domain 1], [domain 2] across [industry/markets/notable employers]`.
- Lines 2–3 = 2–3 signature achievements with metrics, chosen for relevance to the target role.
- Optional line = differentiator/narrative ("Former engineer turned enterprise sales leader", rare skill combination, notable recognition).
- No objective statements ("seeking a challenging role…"); no unbacked soft-skill lines ("excellent communicator, team player"). Every adjective must be earned by a fact.
- Include 2–5 of the job ad's key terms naturally [24][30][41].

## 5. Key Skills
- 6–15 items, mostly hard/functional skills, 1–4 words each, ordered by relevance to the target role.
- Mirror the job ad's exact terminology where truthful (e.g., ad says "stakeholder management" → use that, not "relationship building") — ATS search by skills from the JD [2]. Do not copy whole sentences from the ad [8].
- Technical roles: group into labelled lines (`**Languages:** Python, SQL, TypeScript`, `**Cloud:** AWS (Lambda, ECS), Terraform`). Spell out acronyms once where ambiguous.
- Soft skills belong in evidence (bullets, letter), not as bare list items [42].

## 6. Professional Experience
Role heading: `### Job Title | Employer | City, Country | Mon YYYY – Mon YYYY` (dates right-aligned by renderer). Spell titles out in full (not "Sr. Acct Exec") [27].

Per role:
1. **Optional employer descriptor** (one italic line) when the employer is not widely known: industry, size, footprint.
2. **Scope bullet first** — recruiters often read only the first bullet. One line capturing: team size, budget/revenue/portfolio, geography, client/user base, reporting line. Template: `Lead [team of N] delivering [remit] for [N clients/users/markets], managing [budget/P&L]; report to [role].`
3. **Responsibilities**: 2–5 bullets for the current role, 1–3 for older ones; each ≤2 lines; start with a strong verb.
4. **Key Achievements** sub-block (bold line): 2–5 bullets for recent roles, 1–2 for older ones, each using the XYZ formula (see `bullet-writing.md`). Separating achievements from duties makes value-add visible.
- Promotions at one employer: stack titles with their own dates under one employer; shared bullets.
- Tense: present for current role, past for previous roles; no "I/my".
- Roles older than ~10–15 years or irrelevant: compress to one line (title, employer, dates) or an "Earlier Career" line.
- Age-bias note: candidates 50+ in the US, UK, Australia and New Zealand may leave out graduation years for degrees more than ~15 years old, and a "Selected earlier roles" line is fine. Age discrimination in callbacks is documented [57]; the date advice itself is convention, unsourced. In Germany, France and other markets that expect a complete timeline, keep dates complete. Never alter or hide dates.
- Gaps > 6 months: one honest line if useful ("Career break (2023–2024); completed AWS certification"). Giving a reason is optional. You need not disclose health, disability, pregnancy or caregiving; the user decides (see "Sensitive disclosures" in `regional-conventions.md`). Never hide a gap with fake dates.

## 7. Education & Certifications
- `### Degree, Field | Institution | Country | Year` — add honours/Dean's list/thesis as a bullet only if notable or recent.
- Experienced candidates in the US, UK, Australia and New Zealand: omit GPA and secondary school (convention, unsourced). India, Singapore, Germany and France often expect degree grades, so ask. Graduation years: see the age-bias note in section 6.
- Students/graduates: add GPA if strong, relevant modules, capstone/thesis, awards, exchange; expected graduation date.
- Certifications: `Name — Issuer, Year` (or "In progress, expected Mon YYYY"); put a career-defining licence (CPA, PE, RN, Bar) near the top or in the headline.

## 8. Additional Information
Only items that help: languages with proficiency; tools not already listed; work authorisation (cross-border); availability/notice (market-dependent); publications/patents count; distinctive interests (1 line, optional). Omit "References available upon request" by default [74].

## 9. Length & formatting
- Length by stage: student/early 1 page (US) or 1–2 (elsewhere); mid 2; senior/exec 2 (UK and Germany expect ≤ 2 [42][44]; a third page is an unsourced senior/executive convention elsewhere); academic unlimited [7][8][30][42][45].
- Cut anything older than ~15 years unless critical; quality beats completeness. Where a complete timeline is expected (see the age-bias note in section 6), compress old roles to one line instead of dropping them.
- Single column, standard headings, 10–12 pt body, margins of at least 0.75 in (Harvard [30]), consistent date format, one font family [30][31].
- Never add hidden text, white-font keywords, or prompt-injection text — it is detectable and treated as deception [32].
